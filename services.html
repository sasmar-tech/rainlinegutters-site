Installation

Seamless aluminum K-style gutters — 5" and 6"

K-style is the most common gutter profile in Tampa Bay for good reason: its flat back sits tight against the fascia, its decorative front reads like crown molding, and its shape holds more water than a half-round of the same width.

We roll every run on site from a continuous aluminum coil, cut to the exact length of your roofline. That means no joints along the run — the only seams are at corners and end caps, where we seal them by hand. Fewer seams means fewer drips, fewer separations, and fewer places for water to sit.

Color-matched aluminum

Aluminum doesn't rust, handles salt air better than galvanized steel, and comes pre-finished from the factory in a wide range of colors. We'll match your trim, fascia, or roof — or contrast them on purpose if that's the look you want. Downspouts, elbows, and end caps are finished to match.

5" or 6"? It depends on your roof, not a rule of thumb.

Tampa Bay summer storms routinely deliver short, intense bursts of rain. Gutter size has to handle the peak, not the average.

5" vs 6" K-style at a glance

Typical fitModerate roof areas, simple rooflines, lower pitchLarge roofs, steep pitches, long runs, valley outlets

We size each run after measuring roof area and pitch, and we'll note any spots — like where two valleys dump into one corner — that need extra capacity even if the rest of the house doesn't.

Installation

Half-round gutters

Half-round gutters have a clean, rounded trough that suits historic homes, Old Florida cottages, Mediterranean-style houses, and coastal architecture. They're a design choice as much as a drainage one.

Because a half-round holds less water than a K-style of the same width, we size them carefully and pair them with round downspouts and well-placed outlets. We'll be upfront if your roof needs more capacity than a half-round can comfortably provide.

  • Traditional profile for Hyde Park, Seminole Heights-style bungalows, and waterfront homes
  • Matching round downspouts and fittings
  • Fewer debris pockets thanks to the smooth, rounded interior
  • Mounted with hangers rated for coastal wind

Drainage

Downspouts & drainage

A gutter is only as good as its outlets. Too few downspouts, or downspouts that are too small, and water backs up and spills over the front edge in exactly the storms you bought gutters for.

We place downspouts based on roof area and run length, upsize to 3"×4" where volume calls for it, and finish each one with an extension or splash block that sends water away from your slab, stem wall, pavers, and planting beds.

  • 2"×3" and 3"×4" rectangular, or round for half-round systems
  • Extensions sized to clear foundations and landscaping
  • Placement planned around walkways, pool decks, and AC pads
  • Added downspouts for existing systems that overflow

Protection

Gutter guards & leaf guards

Live oak leaves, pine needles, palm debris, and shingle grit fill Florida gutters faster than most homeowners expect. A clogged gutter in an afternoon storm overflows just like having no gutter at all — except now the water is also sitting against your fascia.

There's no single guard that's best for every house. Fine micro-mesh handles pine needles and oak tassels; sturdier screen or hood-style guards suit homes under broad-leaf trees. We'll look at what's actually overhead and recommend accordingly.

Guards reduce cleaning — they don't eliminate it. Every guard still needs an occasional rinse or brush-off. Anyone promising "never clean again" is overselling.

  • Installed on new Rainline systems or many existing gutters in good condition
  • Fastened to resist wind uplift
  • Chosen to work with your roof edge, not against it

Service

Gutter repairs

Plenty of gutter problems are fixable: a leaking corner, a run that's lost its pitch, a few failed spikes, a crushed downspout, an end cap that let go. We'll repair what's worth repairing.

Repair or replace?

We recommend replacement when the fixes would cost more than they're worth or won't last — for example, sectional gutters leaking at many joints, widespread sagging, corroded steel, undersized gutters that overflow every storm, or fascia rot along most of the run. You'll get an honest call, in writing, with both options when both make sense.

  • Corner and end-cap resealing
  • Re-pitching sagging or ponding runs
  • Replacing spikes with hurricane-rated hidden hangers
  • Downspout replacement, rerouting, and additions
  • Post-storm damage assessment

Service

Gutter cleaning

Trees in Tampa Bay don't take the winter off. Many homes need cleaning at least twice a year, and homes under oaks or pines often need more. A clean-out before hurricane season begins June 1 is one of the simplest storm-prep steps you can take.

Our cleanings cover the whole system: troughs cleared and bagged, downspouts flushed and checked for flow, and a quick look at hangers, seams, and fascia while we're up there.

  • Hand-cleared troughs, debris hauled off — not blown into your beds
  • Downspouts flushed and tested
  • Notes on loose hangers, leaks, or fascia concerns
  • Pre-season and post-storm visits

Structure

Fascia & soffit

Overflowing gutters soak the fascia board behind them. In Florida humidity, wet wood rots quickly — and rotted fascia can't hold a gutter, a hanger, or a storm. Damaged soffit also gives wasps, rodents, and wind-driven rain a way into the attic.

We check fascia and soffit at every inspection. If boards are soft, stained, or separating, we replace them before new gutters go up, then wrap or finish them so they're protected going forward.

  • Rotted fascia board replacement
  • Aluminum fascia wrap, color-matched to your gutters
  • Vented and solid soffit repair or replacement
  • Coordinated with your gutter install — one crew, one schedule

Storm readiness

Hidden & hurricane-rated hangers

The old spike-and-ferrule method drives a nail through the gutter into the fascia. Over a few seasons of heat, rain weight, and wind, those spikes back out — and gutters start to sag, pull away, or come down in a storm.

We use hidden hangers that clip inside the gutter lip and screw into solid fascia, installed at tight spacing for wind and water load. They're invisible from the ground and hold the gutter's shape and pitch far better than spikes.

Already have gutters? A hanger upgrade on an otherwise sound system is often an affordable way to get it ready for hurricane season. Ask about it when you request a quote.

  • Screw-fastened, not nailed
  • Tighter spacing for Florida wind exposure
  • Installed into sound fascia — we replace rotted boards first
